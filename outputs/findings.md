# Findings: NYC Efficiency Scan (FY2023 to FY2026)

This is an independent analysis of public data. It is not an audit and not an official finding.

## Pane A: overtime deep dives

Overtime here means payments for hours worked beyond the normal schedule (the MMR line "Overtime paid").

### NYPD overtime peaked in FY2025 and came down in FY2026, but still outpaces the budget

Overtime went from $871.2M in FY2023 to a peak of $1,135.6M in FY2025, then fell 15.0% to $965.7M in FY2026. Over the full window it is still up 10.8%, against 8.2% growth in total spending, and it remains 14.0% of the department budget. The FY2026 drop is the first sign of a turn. One year does not yet make a structural change, which is the bar Executive Order 12 sets.

*Values are as reported in each year's MMR. Later reports can restate a prior year, so year-over-year changes may differ slightly from the latest MMR tables.*

### DOC overtime kept climbing and is now over a quarter of the budget

Overtime rose 24.5% from FY2023 to FY2026 ($296.4M to $369.1M), while total spending grew only 1.6%. FY2026 added another 8.2% on top of FY2025. Overtime's share of the budget went from 21.8% to 26.7%. Unlike NYPD, there is no turn yet. This is the clearest recurring cost pressure in the scan.

*Staffing levels, absence rates and court-ordered requirements need internal data to explain.*

### DOF's FY2025 overtime spike reversed in FY2026

Through FY2025, DOF overtime had grown 142.5% ($2.1M to $5.1M). In FY2026 it fell back to $2.0M, 5.2% below FY2023. The spike looks like a one-year event rather than a trend. That is a useful result in itself: a second year of data can clear an outlier as well as confirm one.

*DOF reports overtime in thousands of dollars. Values here are converted to millions for comparison.*

### DSNY overtime jumped 60% in FY2026 alone

Sanitation overtime was roughly flat through FY2025 ($165.6M to $201.0M), then rose 60.5% to $322.7M in FY2026, the largest one-year jump among big-budget agencies. Over four years overtime grew 94.9% against 23.0% for total spending, and its share of the budget went from 8.6% to 13.7%. The budget itself also grew fast in FY2026, which points to expanded service rather than a pure overtime problem. Whether it is a one-year surge or a new baseline is the question FY2027 will answer.

*Sanitation overtime swings with snow season and service expansions. One year of growth is a question, not a trend.*

### What adding FY2026 changed

**CCHR entered the zone once FY2026 was added.** Through FY2025, spending was +8.5% and the median outcome trend +45.6%. Through FY2026, spending is +13.7% and outcomes -11.9%. The largest moves in the wrong direction: training and outreach sessions (131 to 27) and complaints successfully mediated (2 to 1).

**DOB entered the zone once FY2026 was added.** Through FY2025, spending was -3.1% and the median outcome trend -14.2%. Through FY2026, spending is +5.1% and outcomes -35.8%. The largest moves in the wrong direction: average days to complete first plan review, minor renovations, initial applications (3.5 to 15.8) and average days between electrical inspection request and inspection (4.2 to 15.9).

**HPD left the zone once FY2026 was added.** Through FY2025, spending was +46.6% with a median outcome trend of -7.7%. Through FY2026, outcomes moved to +12.1% with spending at +39.8%. The added cost now comes with measurable improvement.

**DYCD left the zone once FY2026 was added.** Through FY2025, spending was +15.9% with a median outcome trend of +1.2%. Through FY2026, outcomes moved to +16.0% with spending at +27.2%. The added cost now comes with measurable improvement.

**DOF left the zone once FY2026 was added.** Through FY2025, spending was +4.7% with a median outcome trend of -0.3%. Through FY2026, outcomes moved to +2.3% with spending at +9.1%. The added cost now comes with measurable improvement.
