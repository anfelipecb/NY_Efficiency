# Knowledge card: Executive Order 13, Protecting New Yorkers from Abusive Immigration Enforcement

**In one line:** signed February 6, 2026, it limits how federal immigration authorities can use city property and city data, orders compliance audits at six agencies, and creates an Interagency Response Committee **chaired by the First Deputy Mayor**.

## Why it is in this folder

EO 13 is not an efficiency order. It matters for this role because:

1. The **First Deputy Mayor chairs the Interagency Response Committee (IRC)**, so it is part of the office's actual cross-agency workload.
2. Its audit is a worked example of the office's method: give agencies a deadline, collect structured findings, adopt recommendations, then issue citywide guidance.
3. It connects directly to **service reach**. The mayor's own framing: "No New Yorker should be afraid to apply for city services like child care because they are an immigrant." Fear of data sharing reduces take-up of benefits people qualify for.

## What the order requires

| Section | Requirement | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| §2 Public safety audits | ACS, DOC, DOHMH, DOP, DSS, and NYPD audit all policies on cooperation with federal immigration authorities, under Admin. Code §§ 9-131, 9-205, 10-178, 14-154 | Report to the Mayor by **May 7, 2026** (90 days) |
| §3 City lots | Non-city enforcement personnel cannot use city lots as staging, processing, or operations bases unless there is a formal agreement, a **judicial warrant**, exigent circumstances, or another listed exception | Immediate |
| §6 Privacy | Every agency names a privacy officer (by Feb 20, 2026). The Chief Privacy Officer trains them on the Identifying Information Law (by Apr 7, 2026) | 14 and 60 days |
| Data | Information collected for city purposes is not shared with federal immigration authorities except as required by law | Ongoing |
| Coordination | Creates the **Interagency Response Committee** for whole-of-government crisis response | Ongoing |

## The audit (published May 22, 2026)

- The Mayor received the audit on May 7, 2026. H+H joined voluntarily; NYC Public Schools ran its own parallel review.
- The IRC is chaired by the First Deputy Mayor, with the Chief Counsel, the **Special Advisor for Strategic Coordination and Operations to the First Deputy Mayor**, and the MOIA Commissioner.
- Key findings: agencies reported more interactions with federal immigration authorities since January 2025, including a sharp rise in enforcement activity at homeless shelters between January and August 2025. The IRC also found **significant gaps in agency reporting**: some incidents never reached MOIA's published reports.
- Adopted actions: agency-specific policy updates (to be published on agency websites), updated citywide reporting guidance issued by the First Deputy Mayor, and updated guidance on non-local law enforcement access to city property.

## How it connects to this project

- **Reach pane:** take-up depends on trust. The FY2026 MMR also shows SNAP recipients down 7% after federal H.R.1 tightened eligibility for some immigrant groups. Policy that reduces fear and policy that changes eligibility both show up in the same take-up numbers, and an analyst has to separate the two.
- **Data quality:** the audit's finding that incidents went unreported is a measurement problem. You cannot manage what agencies do not report consistently.

## Interview angles

- "EO 13's audit is a good model of how the First Deputy Mayor's office runs cross-agency work: a fixed deadline, a common template, findings that turn into citywide guidance. I would apply the same structure to efficiency projects."
- "When I look at benefit take-up, I would not read a drop as an efficiency failure without checking for chilling effects and federal eligibility changes first."
