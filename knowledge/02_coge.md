# Knowledge card: Commission on Government Efficiency (COGE) reports

**In one line:** the 2026 Charter Revision Commission that turned public testimony about slow, burdensome city processes into five Charter amendments on the **November 3, 2026** ballot.

## What it is

- A **Charter Revision Commission**: a temporary body that reviews the City Charter and proposes amendments. Nothing it proposes takes effect unless voters approve it in a citywide referendum.
- Convened by Mayor Mamdani in **May 2026** to review the whole Charter for ways to improve efficiency and modernize government.
- Chair: **Patrick Gaspard**. Executive Director: **Ann Cheng**. Vice Chair: Emma Wolfe. Members include Henry Garrido (DC 37), Marc Shaw (former First Deputy Mayor and Budget Director), Ruth Messinger, and Dawn Pinnock (former DCAS Commissioner).
- After the election, COGE is set to become a **permanent advisory board** that keeps collecting New Yorkers' ideas on efficiency reforms.

## The reports

| Date | Document | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| June to July 2026 | 10 public hearings in all five boroughs | 1,220 attendees, 264 live testimonies, 603 written testimonies |
| July 2, 2026 | Preliminary Staff Report | Summarized testimony and agency recommendations |
| July 23, 2026 | Adopted Final Report (unanimous) | Five Charter amendments plus internal agency reforms |

Four themes organized the testimony: **unlocking public space, acceleration, modernization, and fiscal stability.**

## The five ballot questions (November 3, 2026)

| # | Topic | What changes | Claimed effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Public space | One combined application for sidewalk cafés and roadway dining; modernized public notice; covers ramps and benches | Outdoor dining approval from 8 months to about 2; up to $1,800 saved per application |
| 2 | Contracting | Simpler pre-contract questionnaire; quarterly Procurement Policy Board meetings; Mayor can delegate some contract approvals to agencies; public comment period instead of a hearing for awards | Up to 30 days saved per procurement |
| 3 | Street safety and city property | DOT Commissioner can fast-track urgent street safety work; faster sale or lease of small city lots, air rights, and office space | Street safety projects at least 33% faster; small lot sales from 7 months to 90 days; over $200M in revenue from surplus lots |
| 4 | Building permits | A central permitting hub; more DOB divisions can issue certificates of occupancy; waterfront permitting moves to DOB | 40 approvals from 18 offices in one place |
| 5 | Rainy day fund | Reserve target of **12% of city tax revenues**; OMB, consulting the Comptroller, sets a mandatory deposit formula | Long-term fiscal stability |

## Internal reforms that do not need a vote

- Move nonprofit City Council discretionary awards from procurement to grants (pilot: time to payment from 10 months to 6).
- Competitively procured **umbrella agreements** with pre-vetted nonprofits (procurement from 13 months to 6).
- OMB service standards: review standard hires and promotions within **14 days**, capital project initiation applications within **40 days**.

## The framing to notice

COGE was explicit that "speed cannot come at the cost of labor standards, safety or quality," and that real efficiencies come from listening to workers' expertise. A DC 37 leader sat on the commission. Efficiency here means **process time and administrative burden**, not headcount cuts.

## How it connects to this project and the role

- COGE is the "systems" side of efficiency: fix the rules and processes once so every future transaction is faster. That is the same logic as the Reach pane's auto-enrollment argument for Fair Fares.
- If the questions pass, someone has to **implement** them across agencies: a permitting hub spanning 18 offices, new procurement delegation, OMB service standards. That is cross-agency project management, which is the job description.
- Every claimed improvement is a measurable baseline (8 months, 13 months, 7 months). Tracking whether they are achieved is a natural dashboard.

## Interview angles

- "COGE's numbers are promises with baselines. I would want to stand up the tracking for them on day one, so in a year we can say whether outdoor dining really takes two months."
- "Most of COGE is about time and burden, not dollars. That matches how I think about efficiency: the cost to the resident or the small business counts, not only the cost to the budget."
